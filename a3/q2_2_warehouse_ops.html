Q2.2 — Warehouse Delays & Damage

Delayed shipments to stores and damaged items during handling. Root cause analysis, data mart design, and solution architecture.

The two problems

Problem A: Delayed shipments

"There were frequent complaints from retailers of delayed shipment of items." Stores place weekly indents (orders) to their regional warehouse. The warehouse picks, packs, and ships. Items arrive late — or at unpredictable times. Store managers can't plan, shelves sit empty, customers encounter stockouts even when the warehouse has stock.

Problem B: Damaged items

"The company experienced many cases of damaged items. This normally occurs during product shipments or material handling inside the warehouse leading to increased shrinkage." Acme's shrinkage is 1.2-1.5% of total sales (target: 0.6%). For a Rs 3,300Cr revenue company, 1.2% shrinkage = Rs 39.6Cr in losses. Reducing to 0.6% saves Rs 19.8Cr annually.

The key sentence from the case

"The company was unable to clearly identify the root cause of the problem or bottlenecks to take concrete actions to improve the situation." — This is a data visibility problem. The operations are happening, the delays and damage are happening, but there's no measurement infrastructure to pinpoint where.

Root cause analysis — delays

Root causeWhy it happens at AcmeHow data reveals it
1. Picking bottleneck Warehouse receives all store indents on the same day (weekly indent policy). Peak picking volume on indent day, idle the rest of the week. Staff and equipment insufficient for peak day. Timestamp analysis: if picking_start_time clusters heavily on one day and picking_duration spikes, picking is the bottleneck.
2. Packaging delays Electronics require careful packaging (TVs, refrigerators, laptops). If packaging materials run out or trained staff are unavailable, orders queue. Gap between picking_end_time and packaging_start_time reveals wait time. Long gaps = packaging queue.
3. Transport capacity Third-party transporters may not have capacity on the day items are ready. Acme coordinates with multiple transport providers — scheduling mismatches cause items to sit packed but unshipped. Gap between packaging_end_time and shipping_time. If items sit packed for 2+ days, transport scheduling is the bottleneck.
4. Transporter performance Some transporters consistently deliver late. Without SLA tracking data, Acme can't identify which ones. No performance-based consequences. delivery_time per transporter vs SLA. Rank transporters by on-time %. Identify the worst performers.
5. Last-mile to store Remote stores in smaller cities may have fewer delivery options. Items arrive at the regional hub but take days to reach the final store. Transit time correlated with store location / city tier. Stores in Tier 2/3 cities may have systematically longer delivery times.
6. Indent timing mismatch Weekly indent policy means a store that runs out on Tuesday waits until the next indent day. Even if the warehouse processes instantly, the policy creates a built-in 1-7 day delay. Stockout_date vs next_indent_date gap analysis. Average wait time between stockout event and replenishment order.

Root cause analysis — damage

Root causeWhere in the chainHow data reveals it
1. Warehouse handling During picking, moving from storage to packing area. Forklifts, manual handling of heavy items (refrigerators, washing machines, TVs). Damage reported at packaging inspection (before shipping) → damage happened inside the warehouse.
2. Inadequate packaging Packaging step — insufficient cushioning, wrong box size, fragile items not marked. Damage reported at store receiving (after shipping) but correlated with specific product categories (fragile electronics) → packaging issue.
3. Transport damage During shipment — rough roads, improper loading, no temperature control for heat-sensitive items. Damage rate per transporter. If one transporter has 3x the damage rate of others, it's their handling. Damage rate per route (city pair) — rough road segments.
4. Store receiving Unloading at the store — damage during unpacking or moving to shelf locations. Damage discovered days after receipt (during physical verification) → likely happened during in-store handling, not shipping.
5. Product-specific fragility Some SKUs are inherently more prone to damage — large screens (TVs), glass cooktops, precision cameras. Damage rate per product category. High-damage categories need special handling protocols.

The case asks: "What is the average time taken for the individual activities (picking, packaging, shipping, transportation, delivery) in the order fulfilment pipeline?" and "What are the key bottleneck areas?" — this pipeline IS the data model.

Star schema — Order Fulfillment Data Mart

Fact table: fact_order_fulfillment

-- Grain: one row per shipment line item (one product in one shipment)
CREATE TABLE fact_order_fulfillment (
  fulfillment_key     INT NOT NULL AUTO_INCREMENT,
  indent_date_key     INT NOT NULL,        -- FK → dim_date (when store placed indent)
  ship_date_key       INT NOT NULL,        -- FK → dim_date (when shipped from warehouse)
  delivery_date_key   INT NOT NULL,        -- FK → dim_date (when arrived at store)
  store_key           INT NOT NULL,        -- FK → dim_store (destination)
  warehouse_key       INT NOT NULL,        -- FK → dim_warehouse
  product_key         INT NOT NULL,        -- FK → dim_product
  transporter_key     INT NOT NULL,        -- FK → dim_transporter

  -- Time measures (hours)
  indent_to_pick_hrs  DECIMAL(6,1),      -- wait time: indent → picking starts
  picking_hrs         DECIMAL(6,1),      -- picking duration
  pick_to_pack_hrs    DECIMAL(6,1),      -- wait time: picking done → packing starts
  packing_hrs         DECIMAL(6,1),      -- packing duration
  pack_to_ship_hrs    DECIMAL(6,1),      -- wait time: packed → handed to transporter
  transit_hrs         DECIMAL(6,1),      -- in-transit time
  total_lead_time_hrs DECIMAL(6,1),      -- indent → delivery total

  -- Quantity and quality measures
  quantity_ordered    INT NOT NULL,
  quantity_shipped    INT NOT NULL,
  quantity_received   INT NOT NULL,       -- may be less if damaged
  quantity_damaged    INT DEFAULT 0,
  damage_stage        VARCHAR(20),        -- 'warehouse'|'transit'|'receiving'|'unknown'
  sla_target_hrs      DECIMAL(6,1),       -- contractual SLA for this route
  sla_met             TINYINT,            -- 1 if total_lead_time <= sla_target

  PRIMARY KEY (fulfillment_key),
  FOREIGN KEY (indent_date_key) REFERENCES dim_date(date_key),
  FOREIGN KEY (ship_date_key) REFERENCES dim_date(date_key),
  FOREIGN KEY (delivery_date_key) REFERENCES dim_date(date_key),
  FOREIGN KEY (store_key) REFERENCES dim_store(store_key),
  FOREIGN KEY (warehouse_key) REFERENCES dim_warehouse(warehouse_key),
  FOREIGN KEY (product_key) REFERENCES dim_product(product_key),
  FOREIGN KEY (transporter_key) REFERENCES dim_transporter(transporter_key)
);

New dimensions (not shared with sales mart)

CREATE TABLE dim_warehouse (
  warehouse_key    INT NOT NULL,
  warehouse_id     VARCHAR(10),
  warehouse_name   VARCHAR(50),
  city             VARCHAR(30),
  state            VARCHAR(30),
  region           VARCHAR(10),
  capacity_sqft    INT,
  PRIMARY KEY (warehouse_key)
);

CREATE TABLE dim_transporter (
  transporter_key  INT NOT NULL,
  transporter_id   VARCHAR(10),
  transporter_name VARCHAR(50),
  service_type     VARCHAR(20),  -- 'express'|'standard'|'economy'
  sla_hrs          DECIMAL(6,1), -- contracted SLA in hours
  contract_start   DATE,
  contract_end     DATE,
  PRIMARY KEY (transporter_key)
);
Key design decisions

Multiple date keys: Three role-playing date dimensions (indent_date, ship_date, delivery_date) allow analysis of delays at each stage. "What's the average wait between indent and shipment?" is a simple AVG(ship_date_key - indent_date_key).

Pipeline stage durations as measures: Breaking total_lead_time into stage-level durations enables bottleneck identification. The case explicitly asks for this: "average time taken for individual activities."

damage_stage as a degenerate dimension: Records WHERE in the pipeline damage occurred. Critical for root cause attribution.

Diagnostic queries

-- Where is the bottleneck? Average hours per pipeline stage
SELECT
    ROUND(AVG(indent_to_pick_hrs), 1) AS avg_indent_to_pick,
    ROUND(AVG(picking_hrs), 1)         AS avg_picking,
    ROUND(AVG(pick_to_pack_hrs), 1)    AS avg_pick_to_pack_wait,
    ROUND(AVG(packing_hrs), 1)         AS avg_packing,
    ROUND(AVG(pack_to_ship_hrs), 1)    AS avg_pack_to_ship_wait,
    ROUND(AVG(transit_hrs), 1)         AS avg_transit,
    ROUND(AVG(total_lead_time_hrs), 1) AS avg_total
FROM fact_order_fulfillment;
-- Transporter SLA performance — the case asks for this directly
SELECT
    t.transporter_name,
    t.service_type,
    t.sla_hrs AS contracted_sla,
    COUNT(*) AS shipments,
    ROUND(AVG(f.transit_hrs), 1) AS avg_transit,
    ROUND(MIN(f.transit_hrs), 1) AS min_transit,
    ROUND(MAX(f.transit_hrs), 1) AS max_transit,
    ROUND(SUM(CASE WHEN f.sla_met = 1 THEN 1 ELSE 0 END)
        / COUNT(*) * 100, 1) AS on_time_pct
FROM fact_order_fulfillment f
JOIN dim_transporter t ON f.transporter_key = t.transporter_key
GROUP BY t.transporter_name, t.service_type, t.sla_hrs
ORDER BY on_time_pct ASC;
-- Delivery time trends by month, store, and category — the case asks for this
SELECT
    d.year, d.month_name,
    s.city, p.category,
    ROUND(AVG(f.total_lead_time_hrs), 1) AS avg_lead_time,
    ROUND(AVG(f.transit_hrs), 1) AS avg_transit
FROM fact_order_fulfillment f
JOIN dim_date d    ON f.indent_date_key = d.date_key
JOIN dim_store s   ON f.store_key = s.store_key
JOIN dim_product p ON f.product_key = p.product_key
GROUP BY d.year, d.month, d.month_name, s.city, p.category
ORDER BY d.year, d.month, avg_lead_time DESC;
-- Damage analysis — where is damage occurring?
SELECT
    f.damage_stage,
    p.category,
    COUNT(*) AS incidents,
    SUM(f.quantity_damaged) AS units_damaged,
    ROUND(SUM(f.quantity_damaged) / NULLIF(SUM(f.quantity_shipped), 0) * 100, 2)
        AS damage_rate_pct
FROM fact_order_fulfillment f
JOIN dim_product p ON f.product_key = p.product_key
WHERE f.quantity_damaged > 0
GROUP BY f.damage_stage, p.category
ORDER BY units_damaged DESC;
-- Damage rate by transporter — identify worst offenders
SELECT
    t.transporter_name,
    SUM(f.quantity_shipped) AS total_units,
    SUM(f.quantity_damaged) AS damaged_units,
    ROUND(SUM(f.quantity_damaged) / NULLIF(SUM(f.quantity_shipped), 0) * 100, 2)
        AS damage_rate_pct
FROM fact_order_fulfillment f
JOIN dim_transporter t ON f.transporter_key = t.transporter_key
GROUP BY t.transporter_name
ORDER BY damage_rate_pct DESC;

Source systems

WMS (Warehouse Management System): Records picking, packing, and storage operations. Timestamp for each stage. Each regional warehouse has its own WMS (likely different vendors — Acme's heterogeneity problem).

TMS (Transport Management System): Records shipment hand-off, transit tracking, and delivery confirmation. May come from the transporter's system or Acme's coordination system.

Store receiving system: Records receipt, inspection results, and damage reports at the store level.

Indent system: Records store orders to the warehouse — indent date, quantities requested.

ETL/ELT challenges specific to warehouse data

Timestamp normalization: Different WMS systems may record timestamps in different formats/timezones. Must be normalized to a common standard before calculating durations.

Transporter data integration: Third-party transporters may provide data in different formats (CSV, API, email). Need automated ingestion for each provider.

Damage attribution: Damage discovered at store receiving may have occurred at any earlier stage. The ETL logic must implement attribution rules: if packaging inspection passed and store receiving flagged damage → transit damage. If packaging inspection flagged damage → warehouse damage.

Solutions — delays

1. Pipeline stage visibility dashboard

Immediate win: a Power BI / Tableau dashboard showing average time per pipeline stage, by warehouse, by week. The bottleneck becomes visible for the first time. Warehouse managers can see "picking takes 2 hours, but items sit waiting for packing for 18 hours" — the wait time, not the work time, is the bottleneck.

2. Transporter SLA scorecard

Rank all transporters by on-time %, damage rate, and cost. Share the scorecard with transporters quarterly. Make contract renewals conditional on SLA performance. The data mart makes this possible — today Acme has no way to measure transporter performance.

3. Indent policy optimization

The weekly indent policy creates artificial batching. Analysis may show that twice-weekly indents for high-velocity SKUs (phones, popular TVs) would reduce stockout duration by 50% with manageable logistics overhead. The data mart provides the evidence: which SKUs have stockout events within 3 days of the last indent?

4. Exception-based management

Instead of monitoring every shipment, alert only when a shipment exceeds SLA thresholds. SQL view: WHERE total_lead_time_hrs > sla_target_hrs * 1.2. Warehouse managers focus on exceptions, not the full pipeline.

Solutions — damage

1. Damage attribution by stage

The fact table's damage_stage column enables root cause analysis for the first time. If 60% of damage occurs in transit → focus on transport quality. If 30% occurs in warehouse → focus on handling procedures. Data-driven prioritization.

2. Product-specific handling protocols

Damage rate analysis by product category identifies which products need special handling. If TVs have 5x the damage rate of phones, invest in better TV packaging and handling training. Cost-justify the investment with the damage cost data.

3. Transporter damage penalties

Damage rate per transporter → build into contract terms. Transporters with damage rate above threshold face penalties or contract termination. The data mart provides the evidence for contractual negotiations.

4. Packaging quality audit

If damage_stage = 'transit' is high for specific product categories, run a packaging quality audit. The data mart tells you which products to audit. Compare damage rates before and after packaging changes — controlled measurement.

Tools and platforms

WMS platforms with analytics

SAP Extended Warehouse Management (EWM): End-to-end WMS with built-in analytics. If Acme's ERP units include SAP, EWM integrates directly.

Oracle WMS Cloud: Cloud-native, includes performance dashboards. Good for Acme's multi-warehouse environment.

Manhattan Associates: Market leader for retail WMS. Strong analytics for picking optimization and labor management.

Process mining tools

Celonis: The market leader in process mining. Discovers actual process flows from event log data, identifies bottlenecks, recommends optimizations. Would map Acme's fulfillment pipeline automatically from WMS/TMS timestamps.

uipath.com/process-mining

FourKites: Real-time supply chain visibility. Track shipments, predict ETAs, measure carrier performance. Would give Acme real-time visibility into where every shipment is.

Future scenario

Phase 1 → Phase 3 evolution

Phase 1 (immediate): Build the order fulfillment data mart. Measure pipeline stage durations for the first time. Create transporter SLA scorecard. Identify the top 3 bottlenecks. Implement exception alerts for delayed shipments. Measure damage rate by stage, product, and transporter. This alone enables "concrete actions" — which the case says Acme currently cannot take.

Phase 2 (6-12 months): Process mining on fulfillment event logs — Celonis or similar tool discovers actual process flows, deviations, and bottlenecks automatically. IoT sensors on high-value shipments provide real-time transit condition monitoring. Predictive ETA models: based on historical transit data, predict arrival time when shipment departs warehouse. Store managers can plan receiving staff accordingly.

Phase 3 (12-24 months): Real-time shipment tracking across all transporters, integrated into the data platform. Digital twin of the warehouse: model warehouse layout, staff allocation, and picking routes to optimize throughput. ML-based anomaly detection: automatically flag shipments at risk of delay or damage based on historical patterns (transporter + route + weather + product category). Automated dynamic routing: if a transporter is trending late, reroute to an alternative carrier before the delay impacts the store.

What "good" looks like

Industry benchmark for retail warehouse-to-store lead time: 24-48 hours for same-region delivery. Transporter on-time rate target: 95%+. Damage rate target: below 0.3% of units shipped (Acme is currently at 1.2-1.5% shrinkage, which includes damage). The data mart makes these benchmarks measurable for the first time — you can't improve what you can't measure.
