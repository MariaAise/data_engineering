Crow's Foot Notation

ERD Reference Cheatsheet

01  /  What is Crow's Foot Notation?

Crow's Foot is the dominant industry standard for Logical & Physical ERD diagrams. Each relationship line carries two symbols — one at each end — encoding both cardinality (how many?) and participation (is it required?). It superseded Chen notation for logical/physical modelling and is the default in every major CASE and diagramming tool today.

Cardinality (max) The maximum number of instances that can be associated. Expressed as One (single vertical bar) or Many (crow's foot prongs).
Participation (min) Whether the relationship is mandatory (must exist — bar |) or optional (may exist — circle ○). Also called "modality" or "optionality."
Inner vs. Outer symbol Inner symbol (closest to the entity) = max cardinality (bar = 1, crow's foot = many). Outer symbol = min participation (bar = mandatory, circle = optional).
How to read a line Stand at one entity, look toward the other. The symbol touching the far entity = max. The symbol just outside it = min. Both together form the full constraint.
💡

Golden reading rule: Example line  CUSTOMER ||——O< ORDER  reads as: "Each Customer must have zero-or-many Orders (||= mandatory on Customer, O< = optional many on Order). Each Order must belong to exactly one Customer." Always read left→right AND right→left — each direction gives a different sentence.

02  /  Cardinality & Participation Symbols

Symbols shown on right end of line. Crow's foot tip touches the entity. Outer marker = min (participation), Inner marker = max (cardinality).

||
Inner | = max 1  ·  Outer | = min 1 (required). Most common "one" side.
Optional One (zero or one)
O|
Inner | = max 1  ·  Outer ○ = min 0 (optional). Used for nullable FKs.
Mandatory Many (one or more, 1..*)
|<
Inner crow's foot = max many  ·  Outer | = min 1. Classic "has at least one."
Optional Many (zero or more, 0..*)
O<
Inner crow's foot = max many  ·  Outer ○ = min 0. Most common "many" side.

⚠ Tool variant: Some tools (older Visio, ERwin) show double-bar || specifically for "mandatory" (min=1) vs. single bar for "exactly one" (max=1 only). The single-bar-covers-both convention shown here is the 2024–2026 standard in Lucidchart, draw.io, dbdiagram.io, and Creately. When in doubt, check your tool's legend.

M:N Rule: Many-to-many relationships cannot be implemented directly in a relational DB. Always resolve with a junction / bridge table (e.g. StudentCourse) whose PK is a composite of both FKs. Add payload columns (grade, enrollDate) directly to the junction entity.

PK
Primary Key — Uniquely identifies every row. NOT NULL + UNIQUE. Never change after assignment.
FK
Foreign Key — References a PK in another table. Enforces referential integrity via constraints.
SK
Surrogate Key — System-generated (auto-increment INT or UUID). Stable, implementation-independent.
NK
Natural Key — Real-world identifier (ISBN, SSN). Avoid as PK if it could change or be reused.
CPK
Composite PK — Two or more columns form the PK together. Standard in junction tables.
AK
Alternate Key — Candidate key not chosen as PK. Apply a UNIQUE constraint (e.g. email column).

06  /  Best Practices & Modern Tips

Naming conventions Use singular nouns for entities (CUSTOMER, not CUSTOMERS). SQL attributes: snake_case. Relationship verb in label (Customer places Order). Avoid abbreviations in entity names.
Surrogate keys first Prefer system-generated surrogate PKs (AUTO_INCREMENT INT or UUID v7). Never use email, username, or phone as a PK — they change. Apply UNIQUE constraints for business identifiers instead.
Resolve every M:N early Many-to-many cannot exist in a physical schema. Introduce a junction table with a composite PK (FK₁ + FK₂) at the logical stage. Add payload columns (qty, price, enrollDate) to the junction.
Target 3NF for OLTP Normalise to Third Normal Form: atomic values (1NF), full PK dependency (2NF), no transitive deps (3NF). Intentionally denormalise for OLAP / reporting warehouses only — document every deviation.
Model participation explicitly Decide optionality before writing DDL — it drives NOT NULL constraints and JOIN type (INNER vs LEFT). An unresolved circle vs bar in your ERD = a bug waiting to happen in production queries.
Validate with domain experts Review your ERD using plain language: "Can a Customer exist with no Orders?" Crow's Foot is intentionally readable by non-engineers. Catch business-rule errors before any DDL is written.
Read every line both ways Each relationship line gives two sentences. CUSTOMER ||——O< ORDER: (left→right) "A Customer has zero-or-many Orders." (right→left) "An Order must belong to exactly one Customer."
